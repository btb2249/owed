What kind of money gets forgotten?

When a business owes you money and can't reach you, Texas law makes them hand it over to the state for safekeeping. It sits there under your name until you claim it. Common sources:

Final paychecks & wages Utility & rental deposits Insurance refunds Forgotten bank accounts Store & medical refunds Uncashed checks Security deposits Old royalties & dividends Commissary & trust balances Money owed to someone who passed away

Changed your name, moved houses, or lost a parent years ago? Those are exactly the situations where money gets left behind — and exactly the claims we help sort out, including claims by legal heirs when there's no will.

Straight answers

        
This only works if people trust it, so here are the questions everybody asks — answered the way we'd want them answered.

      

      

        Is this a scam?
        
Fair question — unclaimed-money scams exist, so check us the way you'd check anyone: the money itself is real and verifiable on the state's own website, ClaimItTexas.gov, before you ever deal with us. We never ask for money upfront, never hold your claim money, and never want your SSN or bank login. If any "finder" asks for those things, walk away — including from us.

      

      

        Can't I just do this myself for free?
        
Yes — 100%. Claiming your own money from the Texas Comptroller is free, and the link is right on this page. Owed charges for the hunting and the hand-holding: searching old names and addresses, heir claims with no will, knowing which documents the state will demand, and staying on the case. Plenty of people DIY it. Plenty would rather pay 10% of money they'd never have found.

      

      

        Where does my money actually go?
        
From the Texas Comptroller directly to you. Your claim money never passes through Owed's hands — that's the state's process and our favorite trust line. After you've been paid, you pay Owed the 10% fee. If a rep helped you, Owed pays the rep out of that fee.

      

      

        What does it cost if nothing is found?
        
Nothing. Searches are free, and the fee only exists when money is recovered. No money, no fee, no bill for our time.

      

      

        A relative of mine passed away. Can their money still be claimed?
        
Often, yes — by their legal heirs. It's more paperwork than a simple claim: typically the claim form, the death certificate, and for estates without a will, an Affidavit of Heirship. The state processes these claims too, and guiding families through them is some of the most common work we do. Other legal heirs may share in the claim, which the state sorts out.

      

      

        How long does it take?
        
The Texas Comptroller says claims typically take around 90 days to process once everything is filed correctly. Incomplete paperwork is the main thing that slows claims down — which is most of what we help prevent.

      

      

        How do reps actually get paid?
        
Your earnings sit at pending while a claim is in process, flip to payable when the claimant has been paid by the state and Owed's fee is received, and are marked paid when Owed sends your cut — 2% for referrals, 5% for full-service closes, plus any monthly closer bonus you've earned ($25 at 5 closes, $100 at 10). Rep pay always comes out of Owed's 10% fee; it is never charged to the claimant on top.
